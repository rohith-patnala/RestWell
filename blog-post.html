The 4 stages of sleep — and why you're probably missing the deepest one

Dr. Ravi Menon July 14, 2026 8 min read

Every night, your brain runs through four distinct stages of sleep — a cycle it repeats about five times. Most people focus on total hours, but the quality of those hours depends almost entirely on how much time you spend in the deepest stage.

Stage 1: The drift-off

This is the lightest stage, lasting just a few minutes. Your muscles relax, your eyes move slowly, and you can be woken easily. It's the bridge between wakefulness and real sleep.

Stage 2: The settling

Your heart rate drops, your body temperature falls, and your brain waves slow. You spend about half the night here. It's restorative, but it's not where the deepest recovery happens.

"Deep sleep is when your body does its heaviest repair work — muscle growth, tissue healing, and immune strengthening."

Stage 3: Deep sleep (the one you're missing)

This is the stage that matters most, and it's the one people cut short without realising. Deep sleep is when your body releases growth hormone, repairs tissue, and strengthens your immune system. The problem? It mostly happens in the first half of the night — and it's easily disrupted.

An uncomfortable mattress is one of the most common disruptors. Pressure points cause micro-arousals — tiny awakenings you don't remember but that pull you out of deep sleep. Over a week, that's hours of lost recovery.

Stage 4: REM sleep

Rapid eye movement sleep is when you dream. Your brain is almost as active as when awake, consolidating memories and processing emotions. REM increases in the second half of the night, which is why cutting sleep short costs you REM disproportionately.

What this means for your mattress

A mattress that's too firm creates pressure at the shoulders and hips, causing those micro-arousals. One that's too soft lets your spine sag, which triggers discomfort-based awakenings. The right mattress — matched to your sleep position and body type — keeps you in deep sleep longer.
